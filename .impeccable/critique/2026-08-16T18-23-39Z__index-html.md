## Design Specificity Verdict — AUTHORED (strongly), with two lazy seams

**Design review:** This is unmistakably designed *for this product*, not a category template. Real member photography (a candid Savannah sailboat selfie — varied ages/bodies, genuine laughter, un-stockable). A specific human voice ("Six squares, three miles, zero small talk"; real anchors — Foxy Loxy, Forsyth, Jones St, Starland). A committed, authored system: dark-teal ground + electric-rose script accent in a **custom self-hosted "Sisterhood" font** for the one emotional word per headline ("belong.", "soon", "once"). Values are gender-specific and lived ("Women first, always").

Two seams betray the authorship: (1) event-detail tags are **hardcoded identically on every event** — "Come alone · Street parking nearby · We'll be at the long table" appear on the backyard potluck, the 8am walk, and the flower workshop alike; (2) fallback event cards render as five gray "Photo —" boxes, so first paint reads like a wireframe.

**Deterministic scan:** CLI static scan is near-useless here (index.html is a 22-line SPA shell → 1 finding: `overused-font` montserrat). The **browser-injected detector** ran on all four routes and flagged, consistently site-wide: `low-contrast`, `ai-color-palette` (cyan-on-dark), `dark-glow`, `all-caps-body`, `overused-font`, `kicker-above-heading` / `hero-eyebrow-chip`, `skipped-heading`, `monotonous-spacing`, plus per-route `line-length`, `cramped-padding`, `em-dash-overuse`.

**Where the two agree — treat as real:** the **low-contrast** findings. Measured: `#ef2e9f` pink at **2.4:1** (on teal `#0f5257`), **3.0:1** (on `#0a4247`), **3.6:1** (on cream `#fffaf0`) — all below the 4.5:1 AA floor. The review independently flagged muted cream-on-teal as borderline. This is a genuine accessibility failure regardless of brand intent.

**Where the detector over-fires (largely false positives against a deliberately committed dark aesthetic):** `ai-color-palette` (cyan/teal), `dark-glow`, and `monotonous-spacing` (~4px 93–96% — an artifact of a token system, not real monotony). These are saturated-pattern warnings; the brief here is an authored dark-teal world with real photography and a custom font, so the palette/glow flags do not override it. `overused-font`, `em-dash-overuse`, and the eyebrow-kicker device are stylistic and deliberate — note, don't "fix." The one exception the detector is right about even inside the committed look is contrast.

## Overall Impression

The hardest part is already done: this site has a soul. The photography, the voice, and the anxiety-removing RSVP copy make it feel like a real community rather than a funnel — that's rare and it's the whole ballgame for a Persuade surface. What's holding it back isn't taste; it's **integrity at the two moments that matter most: the money moment and the confirmation.** A paid event can silently fall through to a free RSVP, the seat count is asked twice, and the funnel's final click ("Add To Calendar") does nothing. The single biggest opportunity: make the RSVP/checkout flow as trustworthy as the copy promises.

## What's Working

1. **Authentic member photography + human copy.** The un-stocked hero and voice-driven blurbs are the #1 reason this reads as a real community. Keep this bar.
2. **Anxiety-removing RSVP design.** "Refunds up to 48 hours before, no explanation needed," "no application, no fee, just show up," "we'll seat you in the middle of everything" — textbook reassurance delivered exactly at the decision point.
3. **Confident, restrained visual system.** Teal ground + rose Sisterhood script + generous rhythm, held consistently across every route. Reduced-motion is respected globally.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The money moment can silently become a free RSVP.**
- *Why it matters:* On a paid event the ticket panel promises "Secure checkout — card, Apple Pay, Google Pay… handled by Stripe," but `buyCurrent()` falls through to the free RSVP modal when `stripeUrl` is missing — collecting a "ticket" with no charge and no clarity. The visitor leaves unsure whether they paid or hold a seat. This is the highest-stakes moment on the site.
- *Fix:* If `price > 0` and there's no `stripeUrl`, do not render checkout affordances — show an explicit state ("Ticketing opens soon") and never route a paid RSVP through the free path.
- *Suggested command:* `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] Text contrast fails WCAG AA (hard-measured).**
- *Why it matters:* The rose brand pink `#ef2e9f` is used for text/UI at 2.4:1, 3.0:1, and 3.6:1 — all under 4.5:1. Low-vision users and anyone in Savannah sunlight on a phone lose it. Both assessments landed here independently.
- *Fix:* Reserve the pink for large display/script text (≥24px, where 3:1 applies) and non-text accents; for body/eyebrow/UI text on teal or cream, darken the pink or switch to cream/teal that clears 4.5:1. Re-measure against `tokens.css`.
- *Suggested command:* `/impeccable colorize` (then `/impeccable audit` to verify)

**[P1] Double seat entry — the panel qty is lost, modal resets to 1.**
- *Why it matters:* Set "Seats" to 3 on the ticket panel → open the RSVP modal → "How many of you?" resets to 1. The panel stepper feels decorative, and the visitor re-enters a fact they just gave. The modal field is also unbounded while the panel caps at 6.
- *Fix:* Pass `state.qty` into `openRSVP` and prefill the guests field; reconcile the caps. Better: keep one quantity control, not two.
- *Suggested command:* `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] Peak-end failure — "Add To Calendar" is a dead button (and a bad event id shows the first event).**
- *Why it matters:* The confirmation screen is the emotional *end* of the funnel; one of its two buttons does nothing (`case 'add-calendar': break; // TODO`). Separately, `#event/nonsense` falls back to `state.events[0]` instead of the not-found placeholder — a stress-tester silently sees the wrong event.
- *Fix:* Generate an `.ics` / Google Calendar link from event data, or remove the button until it's real. Guard `eventView` so an unknown id renders the placeholder.
- *Suggested command:* `/impeccable harden`

**[P2] Accessibility: screen readers hear "belongsoon", and the modal has no focus trap.**
- *Why it matters:* Script-accent headings concatenate without a space in the DOM — screen readers announce "Connection thatlasts", "came aloneonce", "happenshere". The RSVP modal sets `aria-modal` but Tab escapes to the page behind it. Detector also flags `skipped-heading` (h1→h3, missing h2) on story/event.
- *Fix:* Add a space (or `aria-label` on the heading) around the script span; add a focus trap + return-focus to the RSVP modal; insert an h2 or adjust levels so headings don't skip.
- *Suggested command:* `/impeccable audit`

**[P2] Event-agnostic hardcoded tags undercut the authenticity everything else earns.**
- *Why it matters:* "Street parking nearby" / "We'll be at the long table" on a *backyard potluck* reads as boilerplate; trust erodes exactly where the site otherwise feels handwritten.
- *Fix:* Make the three tags per-event data, or drop them.
- *Suggested command:* `/impeccable clarify`

**[P2] Mobile: 3-row pinned nav eats ~⅓ of the screen; redundant CTAs.**
- *Why it matters:* At 375px the sticky nav stacks logo / links / "Save My Seat" pill into ~270px and stays pinned while scrolling. Meanwhile hero "See What's Coming Up" + "Come Say Hi", the nav pill, and the closing "See What's Coming Up" all route to Gatherings — several differently-labeled buttons, one destination.
- *Fix:* Collapse the nav to one row on mobile (compact menu, or drop the redundant pill since the links already reach Gatherings); differentiate or reduce the home CTAs to one clear primary.
- *Suggested command:* `/impeccable adapt`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (first-timer):** Clicks "Get My Ticket" on the $24 dinner, is promised Stripe/Apple Pay, and lands on a free "Save your seat" form — *"Did I actually pay? Do I have a seat?"* Also can't tell how the hero's "Come Say Hi" differs from "See What's Coming Up."

**Riley (stress tester):** (a) Paid event with no `stripeUrl` collects an RSVP with no payment. (b) Modal guest count has no max while the panel caps at 6. (c) `#event/nonsense` silently shows the *first* event instead of the not-found placeholder. (d) The confirmation's Add-to-Calendar is a no-op.

**Casey (distracted mobile):** Three-row pinned nav eating ~⅓ of the screen; hero image pushed far below the fold; long stacked home before reaching events; content pops in with no skeleton.

**Sam (accessibility):** (a) Script headings announce as "calendarsoon" / "came aloneonce". (b) No focus trap in the RSVP modal — Tab leaks to the page behind. (c) Muted cream-on-teal + pink text below 4.5:1. (d) The seats value between the +/- buttons isn't associated with them for announcement.

## Minor Observations

- `init()` renders empty, then re-renders with data — content pop-in with no skeleton/loading state; fallback cards read as gray "Photo —" wireframes on first paint.
- Only one testimonial has a real avatar; the others fall back to monogram initials — a visually inconsistent trio.
- Price is expressed in ~4 registers ("$24 · tickets" / "Ticketed" / "Free" / "Free to come" / "Just tell us you're coming"). One vocabulary would read cleaner.
- Gatherings is a flat 5-item chronological list — no month grouping or upcoming/past split as it grows.
- Detector stylistic flags (`em-dash-overuse`, `all-caps` eyebrows, montserrat share) are deliberate voice/system choices — leave them.
- Keep: footer "Women first, always." and the "Wander over" / "Say hi" column heads.

## Questions to Consider

1. At the $24 moment, does she leave *certain* she has a ticket — or hoping she does?
2. If both hero buttons and the nav pill all go to Gatherings, why are there three of them with three labels?
3. Why do we ask how many seats, twice?
4. What does a woman see landing mid-scroll on her phone — and is it right that a third of that screen is navigation?
5. Every event says "Come alone." Brave brand truth, or the same sticky note on all five doors?
